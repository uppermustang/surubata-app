A one-person business does not need an AI department. It needs fewer repeated chores: the proposal you rewrite, the invoice you forget, the caption you stare at, the WhatsApp thread that goes quiet.

This is a Saturday plan. Four hours. Four systems. Nothing here requires a developer, and nothing here should replace the part of the work that is actually you.

## What to automate, and what to leave alone

Automate a step when all three are true.

- You do it more than twice a week.
- The words barely change.
- A mistake is easy to spot before it reaches a client.

Leave these alone.

- The first reply to a new person.
- The price of a custom job.
- Any sentence that has to sound like you on a tired day. A model can draft it. You send it.

If a tool asks you to change how you sell so the tool can function, the tool is the wrong shape.

## The stack

Keep it boring.

1. A single notes document for offers, prices, and the answers you already give. This is the source. Tools read from it. They do not invent policy.
2. A spreadsheet with three columns: name, next step, date. This is the memory. A CRM you will not open is not memory.
3. A runner you already understand — Zapier, Make, or a reminder in your calendar — that only does clerical work.
4. A chat model for drafts. It reads your notes. It does not browse the internet for your prices.

More tools usually means more places for a lead to disappear.

## The Saturday plan

**Hour 1. Write the source.** List your offers, the price range, what you will not do, and five questions clients always ask. Write the answers the way you would say them on a call. Use Nepali, English, or both, matching the client rather than the tool.

**Hour 2. Build the memory.** Every open conversation gets one row. The next step is a verb: send proof, confirm the date, wait until Thursday. If you cannot name the next step, it is not a lead yet.

**Hour 3. Attach one automation.** The best first automation is a reminder, not a message. When a row has not moved for two days, you get a ping. You decide what to send. The machine only refuses to let the row vanish.

**Hour 4. Draft the three templates.** Price. Proof. Still thinking. Keep them short enough to edit. A template you are proud of and never send is a hobby.

## Monday rules

If the draft sounds like a brochure, rewrite the first sentence in the client’s words. If the automation fires and you ignore it twice, delete it. A dead ping trains you to ignore the live ones.

Once a month, fix the source document. Tools amplify whatever you wrote, including the outdated price.

## Where the weekend goes to waste

People connect five apps before they have written the source. They automate the first message and sound like a stranger to the only person who was ready to pay. They buy a seat because a thread called it the new standard.

You do not need the standard. You need the second message to go out tomorrow.
